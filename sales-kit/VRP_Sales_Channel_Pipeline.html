02 — Market Thesis

Where the money moves

Rare disease distribution is not a volume game. Manufacturers buy control: clinical depth, clean data, patient experience, and a pharmacy that will not dilute their brand inside a PBM machine.

Structural tailwind

~Half of recent novel FDA approvals carry orphan designation. Exclusive / limited networks cover roughly one-third of specialty drugs. Independents dominate exclusive rare networks.

Buying moment

Network selection typically locks 12–24 months pre-launch (Phase 3 / BLA-NDA planning). Late-cycle chase loses to incumbents already embedded with the brand team.

Competitive set

Primary independents: PANTHERx Rare, Orsini, AnovoRx, Optime Care / AscellaHealth, Biologics by McKesson. PBM SPs win broader networks; VRP wins exclusivity on rare.

03 — Ideal Customer Profile

Who we pursue — and who we skip

Primary ICP (A-tier)

  • Company: Emerging / mid-size biopharma with ≥1 orphan or ultra-orphan asset in Phase 3, NDA/BLA, or first 24 months post-approval.
  • Product: Rare / orphan indication; specialty dispensing suitable for mail-order or high-touch SP; cold-chain OK; patient population typically <10,000 U.S. (often <1,000).
  • Channel intent: Considering exclusive or limited (≤4) specialty pharmacy network; values independence from PBM ownership.
  • Buying committee: VP/Head of Market Access, Patient Services / Hub Lead, Brand / Commercial Lead, Trade / Distribution, Medical Affairs (influence), Procurement / Legal (late).
  • Trigger events: Positive Phase 3, PDUFA date set, new CCO/CCO commercial hire, prior SP underperformance, hub transition RFP, indication expansion, competitor SP M&A / service issues.

Secondary ICP (B-tier)

  • Larger pharma orphan franchise teams seeking a high-touch independent alongside a PBM SP.
  • Manufacturers needing non-commercial / bridge / PAP adjacency (route through RareMed partnership narrative when appropriate).
  • International biotechs entering U.S. commercial for the first time with lean access teams.

Disqualify / deprioritize

  • High-volume specialty (e.g., broad immunology) where PBM network inclusion is non-negotiable and exclusive is impossible.
  • Retail / open-network oral generics without rare support needs.
  • Opportunities where sole decision criterion is lowest dispensing fee with no clinical/data weighting.

08 — Plays & Messaging

Standard plays the team runs every week

Play A — Pre-launch exclusive lock

  1. Identify asset 12–24 months pre-approval.
  2. Executive-to-executive intro (Gordon / Tim / Justin as appropriate).
  3. Disease-state working session (clinical + access).
  4. Draft exclusive distribution blueprint + KPI scorecard.
  5. Sole-source preference memo for manufacturer internal use.
  6. Contract → implementation war-room → launch day excellence.

Play B — RFP win

  1. Qualify: is exclusive/limited real, or checkbox theater?
  2. Assemble tiger team (BD, clinical, ops, data, quality).
  3. Lead with jockey narrative + independence + rare-only focus.
  4. Submit crisp RFP; offer live scenario simulation.
  5. Reference calls staged by therapeutic adjacency.
  6. BAFO only if strategic; do not race to bottom on fees.

Play C — Displacement / transition

  1. Document incumbent failure modes (data, adherence, HCP complaints).
  2. Present zero-disruption transition plan (phased, patient-level).
  3. Offer parallel operations window; protect PAP / bridge continuity.
  4. Close on risk removal, not price.

Vanscoy Rare is the independent rare pharmacy built by the people who defined rare pharmacy. We give manufacturers exclusive-grade control — clinical depth, clean data, and patient-first execution — without PBM conflict. If the therapy is rare, the pharmacy should be too.
